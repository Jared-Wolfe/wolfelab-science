Essential literature

The WRP system, from foundation to current standard.

These four papers created the cycle-based framework, added explicit molt status, developed its field application, and established today’s global coding system.

Current standard2022

Application of a global age-coding system (“WRP”), based on molts and plumages, for use in demographic and other studies of birds

Pyle, P., M. Gahbauer, E. I. Johnson, T. B. Ryder, and J. D. Wolfe. Ornithology 139:ukab063. doi:10.1093/ornithology/ukab063.

Start here for the current three-character codes, optional adjunct field, coding rules, and recommendations for monitoring programs. This paper supersedes earlier “after” notation where the systems differ.

Read the current standard ↗ Open the PDF ↗

01 · Original system · 2010

Using molt cycles to categorize the age of tropical birds: an integrative new system

Wolfe, J. D., T. B. Ryder, and P. Pyle. Journal of Field Ornithology 81:186–194. doi:10.1111/j.1557-9263.2010.00276.x.

Introduced the biological, cycle-based framework and explained why it can be applied across latitudes and breeding seasons.

Read the original paper ↗

02 · Molt status · 2011

Modifications to a molt-based ageing system proposed by Wolfe et al. (2010)

Johnson, E. I., J. D. Wolfe, T. B. Ryder, and P. Pyle. Journal of Field Ornithology 82:422–424. doi:10.1111/j.1557-9263.2011.00345.x.

Added the middle position that distinguishes active predictable molt (P) from a completed plumage or cycle (C).

Open the paper ↗

03 · Field synthesis · 2012

Using molt and plumage cycles to age tropical birds: updates and recent advances

Wolfe, J. D., T. B. Ryder, P. Pyle, and E. I. Johnson. Ornitología Neotropical 23.

Synthesized early experience with WRP and clarified its practical application to tropical birds in the hand.
