The Wolfe–Ryder–Pyle system

A universal age-categorization system using molt and plumage patterns.

WRP is a universal system for categorizing age using predictable molt and plumage patterns shared by birds worldwide. It replaces calendar-year-based categorization systems while retaining more biological information, remaining flexible across species, and avoiding seasonal constraints. The tools used to determine age—including skulling, gape, feather wear, and other species-specific criteria—remain unchanged.

Why WRP?

Calendar-year systems can separate cohorts when breeding is strongly seasonal, but become difficult to apply when breeding overlaps January 1—as it commonly does in tropical regions, the Southern Hemisphere, and among irregular breeders. Introduced in 2010 and refined through subsequent research, WRP instead asks where a bird is in its progression through molts and plumages. That biological foundation allows the system to work across species, latitudes, and breeding seasons.

A gentle introduction This guide teaches WRP rather than species-specific aging. A basic understanding of feathers, molt limits, molt extent, and molt strategies will help you get the most from it.
